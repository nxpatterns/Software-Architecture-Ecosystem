# Video Platform & Distribution Channel Research

Version: v0.1 - 2024-06-16 - 10 AM - Vienna

## Purpose

This document captures a research session on video distribution platforms, their formats, and strategic use cases. The goal was to build a complete, accurate reference for planning video content across social platforms, investor outreach channels, and async sales tools. It covers what platforms exist, what formats they support, where the terminology is wrong or inconsistent in common usage, and which platforms are actually worth attention for a B2B SaaS context targeting DACH and US investors.

---

## Key Findings

### Terminology Corrections

"Reels" is a Meta trademark. It applies only to **Instagram** and **Facebook**. Calling TikTok videos "TikTok Reels", X posts "X Reels", or LinkedIn content "LinkedIn Reels" is incorrect. Those platforms use their own naming or no special label at all.

LinkedIn has no "Reels" feature. It has a newer short vertical video feature informally called **LinkedIn Short Videos**, but this is not branded as Reels.

### YouTube Competitors

YouTube has no meaningful general-purpose competitor. The landscape:

| Platform | Character | DACH Relevance |
|---|---|---|
| Vimeo | Professional, embed-focused, paid tiers, no ads | Medium (portfolio/client use) |
| Dailymotion | French, smaller | Low |
| Rumble | US, politically right-wing | None for B2B |
| Odysee | Decentralized, libertarian, crypto-adjacent | None |
| Twitch | Gaming/live streaming | None |

### Async Video / Investor Outreach Tools

These are not social platforms -- they are link-based async video tools used heavily in US business contexts.

| Tool | Primary Use | Notable Feature |
|---|---|---|
| Loom | Screen + webcam recording, link share | US standard for async demos and pitches |
| Vidyard | Sales/enterprise video, link share | Analytics: who watched, how long |

**Practical use case:** Record a 3-5 minute product demo, send the link in a cold email to investors. No meeting required as a first step. Vidyard's watch analytics add signal for follow-up timing.

These belong in a separate category from social distribution platforms.

### WhatsApp as a Distribution Channel

WhatsApp is a messenger, not a discovery platform. No algorithm, no reach beyond your contact network.

**Status** (24h ephemeral video, max 30s per clip) is technically viable for a grassroots/guerrilla approach: share a short tour demo, ask friends/contacts to post it as their own Status. Low scale, not automatable, but valid as a warm-start tactic in early stages.

Not suitable for systematic content distribution or investor outreach.

### Platform Priority by Use Case

| Use Case | Top Platforms |
|---|---|
| B2B content distribution (DACH) | LinkedIn, YouTube |
| Short-form reach / awareness | Instagram Reels, TikTok, YouTube Shorts |
| US investor outreach | LinkedIn, X (Twitter), Loom/Vidyard |
| Embed in proposals / client decks | Vimeo |
| Guerrilla / personal network | WhatsApp Status |

**X (Twitter)** is worth noting for US investor relations specifically: since 2022 it has become more active for tech/VC discourse, and many US angels and VCs engage publicly there.

## Some Detailes

- **Rumble / Odysee:** Niche political/ideological audiences. Wrong context for B2B SaaS.
- **Snapchat:** Consumer-only, young demographic, no B2B angle.
- **Pinterest Idea Pins:** Niche use case, low ROI for most B2B scenarios.
- **Dailymotion:** Technically present in EU, but negligible real-world impact.
- **WhatsApp as a broadcast channel:** Works only via manual personal networks, not scalable.
